Problem statement

From configuration selection to policy selection

As long as the decision is evaluated only in the current state, the problem looks one-step. Once the minimum dwell time τd is introduced, that is no longer enough: the chosen configuration must remain admissible throughout the whole dwell interval.

A special case, not the general lawExpand section
One-step criterion

A special case, not the general law

The one-step criterion is correct only when the ordering of configurations by survivability does not change over the dwell interval and no admissible configuration loses admissibility before the next decision epoch.

This condition is often violated under sudden failures and jump-like epistemic degradation.

What trajectory planning buysExpand section
Practical result

What trajectory planning buys

On the reference model, trajectory planning does not improve average survivability under predictable degradation, but in sudden-failure scenarios it reduces the time spent with an inadmissible resource from 3.55% to 1.90%, with only a 0.12% reduction in integral survivability.
